# Layer 1 validation, 2026-08-17

**Status:** first execution of `validation/protocol.md`. Layer 1 only.
**Analyzer commit:** `57f6132`
**Controls:** `idd` at `honest-conversion` (positive) and `develop` (negative), cloned rather than checked out, so the source working tree was untouched.

## Bottom line

The positive control passes. The negative control does not reach its expected threshold.

| control | Slop signals | protocol expects | outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| honest-conversion | 2 | 3 or fewer | **passes** |
| develop | 5 | 7 or more | **misses by 2** |

The instrument separates the two controls, and every discriminating indicator points the right way. It separates them by less than the protocol predicted in April.

**The threshold is not adjusted, and that is the protocol's own instruction:** a deviation may be a real finding about the branch or a calibration problem, and which it is must be determined before anything is changed. Two candidate explanations, neither tested. `develop` may simply be better than the protocol assumed when it was written. Or the eleven-signal count the thresholds were set against predates the current twenty-indicator panel, in which case "7 of 11" does not mean what it meant when written.

## What separates, and by how much

Four indicators carry the separation. L1.5 reads 74.7 Healthy against 23.9 Slop. L1.12, unreachable code, reads 0.84 Healthy against 5.87 Slop. L1.17, god-file concentration, reads 0.85 Not Healthy against 3.27 Slop. L1.18, unbounded mutable state, reads 15.1 against 35.6, both Not Healthy but a clear direction.

## What does not separate, and one of them matters

L1.14 reads exactly 6 on both controls. All twelve findings are `generic-credential`, and every one sits in a test file, a documentation page, a CI workflow or a `.env` template. That is by design rather than a defect: the scanner matches `gitleaks detect --no-git` over git-tracked files, and a credential committed to a test file is committed. The consequence for validation is real all the same. L1.14 contributes nothing to separating these two controls, and the protocol's Layer 1 signal count treats it as one of the eleven.

L1.15 is Slop on both, 17.64 against 18.8, and separates nothing either.

## What was not measured

L1.13 returned n/a because jscpd is not installed on the measuring machine. L1.19 and L1.20 were not run: the audit used `--no-exec`, so neither control's test suite was executed. Layer 2 and Layer 3 are human audits and have not been performed, so this is one layer of three.

## Method

Both controls were cloned with `git clone --single-branch` from the local repository into a temporary directory. The source repository was verified before and after to be on its own unrelated branch with a clean working tree. The analyzer ran at `57f6132` with `--no-exec --format json`.
